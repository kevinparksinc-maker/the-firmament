The +8.8 territorial margin created by the nakshatra modifier may have been real, but a game that goes to penalties should never output 90%+ confidence. Either:
1. The underlying scoring system is overstating separation on matches this close, or
2. The nakshatra modifier is amplifying differences that don't translate to actual match dominance

**Score vs. Real-World Result:**

- **Overstated by: 43.75 percentage points**

### The Real Problem: Base Territorial Control ≠ Real Match Dominance

**What base territorial control measures:**
- Are the favorite's house lords in their own cluster? (Germany: -3, Brazil: -1)
- Are the underdog's house lords in their own cluster? (Paraguay: +5, Norway: +5)

**What it doesn't measure:**
- Actual possession percentage
- Actual shot count / efficiency
- Player quality differences
- Attacking vs defensive tactical setup

**Why this creates false upsets:**
- Germany had 65% possession, 21 shots but -3 territorial (contradiction)
- Brazil likely has similar dominance but -1 territorial
- Yet the system predicts they lose because "territorial control says so"

**The pattern:** Both favorites have Saturn in H3 (fall sign, cadent house) and weak H1 placement, creating a -3 to -1 territorial score. But this chart signature doesn't mean "weak team"—it means "house lord positions happen to favor the underdog's clusters on this date."

### Hypothesis: Territorial Control Is Too Narrow

Territorial control correctly measures cluster dominance but may not correlate perfectly to match dominance in soccer, where:
1. Possession and shots are separate from "territorial control"
2. A team can have weak house lord placement but still dominate possession
3. The home/away advantage and actual team quality aren't in the chart

**Next diagnostic:** Compare predicted territorial control vs. actual possession % for both games. If Germany had 65% possession with -3 territorial, that's a systematic miscalibration—territorial control is measuring something that doesn't match real dominance.
